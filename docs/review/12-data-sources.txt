Import your own wearable data

Imported data uses its own detector settings. The on-board model is set for the full sensor set and, given only a watch's overnight heart rate, alerts far less often than its budget and flags fewer episodes. So an import runs on a heart-rate-only model whose thresholds were calibrated on 251 real people; its expected performance is the "heart rate alone" row on the Validation screen (about one false notice a month), measured mostly on Fitbit wearers; other watches did worse in the real data, in a small sample, and many Apple Watch recordings were too sparse to use. Only heart rate feeds the detector; activity, sleep, HRV and wrist temperature in the file are shown but not used by it; blood pressure, blood sugar, oxygen and thermometer readings in the file are checked against the same fixed lines as typed readings. One more difference: the model was calibrated on resting heart rate computed from raw night-time samples, while an import gives the watch maker's own daily resting heart rate, which is smoothed differently. Tried on Fitbit's own daily values for 63 people in another dataset, it gave about one alert a month at the notice tier, as budgeted, and about a fifth over its budget at the alert tier. Its expected performance comes mostly from Fitbit wearers: for Apple Watch and other devices, detection above luck was not shown (few episodes) and the false-alert rate on their own resting heart rate was not measured, so treat those imports as unvalidated.

Read in this browser only; the file is never uploaded. Keel then learns your baseline from it and shows the same Today and Trends screens for you. Supported: a CSV with a date column and any of rhr, hrv, spo2, temp (a wrist sensor), temperature (a thermometer), sleep_h, ex_min, mood, fluid, mass, sbp, dbp, glucose; dates as YYYY-MM-DD, or day/month/year or month/day/year when the file shows which; Fitbit export JSON (resting heart rate, sleep); Apple Health export.xml. Several readings on one day are averaged, except the most worrying one of the day is kept for blood sugar and blood pressure (the lowest if any is low, otherwise the highest), oxygen (the lowest) and thermometer temperature (the highest). Blood sugar in mmol/L is converted to mg/dL when most of the file is under 35; otherwise a value under 35 is kept, treated as dangerously low and listed so you can check it.

Live heart-rate strap

Connect any Bluetooth chest strap or watch that broadcasts the standard heart-rate service. Lie still for two minutes after waking, then save the reading. It is kept as a spot heart rate and HRV and checked against the fixed safety lines; the detector itself uses overnight values, not spot readings.
