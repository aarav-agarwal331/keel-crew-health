SCRIPTED EVENT
What was scripted: illness (infection-like strain)

This is the day the scripted illness symptoms begin and the simulated crew member ticks them at check-in. An odd tired or congested day before this is ordinary simulated background, not the script. Scripted at 2.15 times the simulator's illness template (fitted to real data; its peak is 3.3 bpm): a peak resting heart rate rise of about 7 bpm. That matches the published median among cases a detector caught, so this is an easy case. The simulated rise starts up to 4 days before the symptom day. Real responses vary widely; in the simulator about three illnesses in ten have no heart-rate rise at all. On any one night, ordinary night-to-night variation of about 3.3 bpm comes on top of the scripted rise.

RESULT OF THIS REPLAY

In this scripted case Keel flagged it with her check-in, on the day symptoms began, with a specific action and no link to Earth.

Sensors alone, or with her check-in? The flag on Day 120 came from the symptoms she ticked at check-in, combined with drifting readings. The sensors alone showed the illness pattern on Day 121, 1 day after symptoms began.

On Day 119 the detector had already moved, but the readings then looked like something else ("Your mood has been lower than usual") and the advice was for that. It is not counted as flagging this event.

Keel's log for this replay also has 6 entries that are not about the scripted event. Some are one open notice being read differently as the days pass; some reflect ordinary lapses in the simulated routine, such as a missed exercise session; some are false alerts. That is part of the picture: notices are budgeted at about one false one per person-month in simulation, and real crews would likely see more.

Across all the real data, not this one script: heart rate alone (not the eleven-channel model in this replay, which is tested in simulation only) flagged 50% of 107 real illness episodes, 22 points more than luck, at about 1.0 false alerts per person-month. Useful early warning was not shown.

The replay runs on past the demo's "today" (Day 121) to show how the readings evolve over the next few days; those days are simulated ahead, and the notice is still open at the end (only the medical officer closes it). One scripted event proves nothing by itself. The day of the scripted illness was chosen from a short list when the demo missions were built, by looking at the detector's output for two quiet weeks beforehand; no candidate day had them, so the first was used and earlier notices appear. This replay is not a random draw.
