# 30-second demo video: script and storyboard

Record at tablet size (820 × 1180, dark theme) from the live site. Screen recording only, captions
burned in, no voice needed (add the voice-over line if the format allows audio). Every shot is a real
screen; the path to reach it is given. Total 30 seconds.

Before recording: open the site, go to **More → Erase my data on this device** so the demo starts
clean, then **More → Mission clock** and drag to **Flight day 101** (a day with a working link).

| # | Time | Screen and action | Caption on screen | Voice-over (optional) |
|---|------|-------------------|-------------------|-----------------------|
| 1 | 0:00–0:04 | **More → Earth link.** Hold on the header showing the one-way delay and the delay chart. | On the way to Mars, a question to Earth and its answer take minutes to most of an hour. Sometimes there is no answer at all. | "On the ISS, a doctor on Earth is seconds away. On the way to Mars, they are not." |
| 2 | 0:04–0:08 | Same screen. Tap **Cut the link now**. The header changes to "No link to Earth" and the top chip turns orange. | So we cut the link. | "So we cut the link." |
| 3 | 0:08–0:14 | **More → Mission replay.** Speed **Fast**, tap **Play**. The three charts draw; the "What was scripted" entry and the alert entry appear in the feed. | Keel learns each crew member's own normal and notices drift. All on the tablet. | "Keel keeps working. It learns each person's own normal and notices when they drift from it." |
| 4 | 0:14–0:19 | Tap **Open that day as Lucía**. Today shows "Act today" and the recommended action, with a field to enter the thermometer reading. Tap **Why, and what next**; scroll to "How soon, and who should know" and "Where this advice comes from". | One action, how soon, who, and the source. No diagnosis. No chatbot. | "It says what changed and gives one action, with its source." |
| 5 | 0:19–0:23 | **Crew** tab. The commander view shows "Talk to the medical officer first" and "Reason not shared". | The crew member owns the data. The commander sees a flag, not the reason. | "The crew member decides who sees what." |
| 6 | 0:23–0:30 | **More → Does it work? Validation.** Hold on "The short version": "+30 points above luck", "+17 points", the false-alert tile, and the box "What these results do not show". | Tested on real wearable data from 251 people: 30 points better than luck at spotting illness, at one false alert a month. One threshold for everyone: 17. And we show what it cannot do. | "We tested it on real data from 251 people, and we show the limits too." |

End card (last second, over shot 6): **Keel — a crew health monitor that keeps working when Earth cannot answer.**

Notes for whoever records it:

- Shot 2 must land before 0:10. Do not trim shot 1 below three seconds; the problem statement needs it.
- The replay result in shot 3 says when Keel flagged the scripted event relative to the scripted
  symptom day (around that day; the card itself warns against reading it as early warning). Do not
  describe it as early warning; the validation does not support that claim.
- Before recording, dismiss nothing on Today: the strip at the top ("Keel is a crew health monitor
  that works with no link to Earth") is a good opening frame if you prefer to start there.
- Every screen that shows mission data carries the "Simulated data" chip. Keep it in frame.
- A phone-width recording also works; the layout is the same in one column.
